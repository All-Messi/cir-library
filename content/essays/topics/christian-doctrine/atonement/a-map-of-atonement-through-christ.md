.5in'><span style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'>This formulation of

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-bottom:0in;text-align:justify'><span
style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'>_________</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-bottom:0in;text-align:justify;text-indent:
.5in'><sup><span style='font-size:14.0pt;line-height:115%'>1</span></sup><span
style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'>2 Corinthians <b>5</b>:18-20</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-bottom:0in;text-align:justify;text-indent:
.5in'><sup><span style='font-size:14.0pt;line-height:115%'>2</span></sup><span
style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'>“The law of the harvest” says that cause
and effect are of the same kind: Matthew<b> 7</b>:16; Luke <b>6</b>:44; John <b>3</b>:6;
James <b>3</b>:12; cp Hosea <b>8</b>:7; 1 Corinthians<b> 15</b>:50.</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-bottom:0in;text-align:justify;text-indent:
.5in'><sup><span style='font-size:14.0pt;line-height:115%'>3</span></sup><span
style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'>The basic format for reconciliation
is (a) repentance by the offender plus (b) forgiveness by the offended. That
removes the past act from any longer affecting the present relationship.</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-bottom:0in;text-align:justify;text-indent:
.5in'><sup><span style='font-size:14.0pt;line-height:115%'>4</span></sup><span
style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'>Repentance itself is a condition laid
down by the offended party; it does not require the one sinned against to
forgive (Hebrews <b>12</b>:16-17). Allowing repentance to have a place is
already a preliminary act of grace. Repentance is repentance only if a person
means it.</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-bottom:0in;text-align:justify;text-indent:
.5in'><sup><span style='font-size:14.0pt;line-height:115%'>5</span></sup><span
style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'>Forgiveness itself is also an act of
grace since repentance is a condition for forgiveness, not a cause of it. That
means that anything further is GRACE +.</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-bottom:0in;text-align:justify;text-indent:
.5in'><sup><span style='font-size:14.0pt;line-height:115%'>6</span></sup><span
style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'>Identification with Christ means
commitment to him in this case because he is Lord. It means commitment to his
values and purposes, which are the same as the Father’s. The equation of
Father-Son is something of another elaboration of the basic reconciliation
process. Normally offenders identify with the ones they have sinned against in
some unloving act. Love is essentially an act of identification with someone by
acting toward the other as “I” would want to be acted toward if I were in their
shoes (as limited, of course, by the general principles of interpersonal
behavior common to all people).</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-bottom:0in;text-align:justify;text-indent:
.5in'><sup><span style='font-size:14.0pt;line-height:115%'>7</span></sup><u><span
style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'>Works</span></u><span
style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'> produce the result; <u>faith/trust</u>
relies on someone else to provide the result. The offender acts in both faith
and works, but the acts have different values. </span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-bottom:0in;text-align:justify;text-indent:
1.0in'><span style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'>A <u>cause</u> is done
to bring about the result; a c<u>ondition</u> is done toward another person,
who gives the result (John <b>3</b>:14-15). The other person is the one that
established that condition in the first place. </span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-bottom:0in;text-align:justify;text-indent:
1.0in'><u><span style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'>Aspiration</span></u><span
style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'> tries to do all though fails to <u>achieve</u>
all. Here is where sanctification by degrees takes place, which is Christian
growth toward complete righteousness</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-bottom:0in;text-align:justify;text-indent:
.5in'><sup><span style='font-size:14.0pt;line-height:115%'>8</span></sup><span
style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'>If people were to do everything all
the time that God expected (*Deuteronomy<b> 27</b>:26 &lt; Galatians <b>3</b>:10),
he would owe it to them to count their perfect righteousness as basis for
friendship (Romans <b>4</b>:1-4). </span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-bottom:0in;text-align:justify;text-indent:
1.0in'><span style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'>Reconciliation is always
a “gift” to the offender by the offended; (a) the one in need of restoration always
has to rely/trust/believe on the offended one’s promise to choose to renew/give
relationship, because repentance—in all its manners of expression—is always an
(appropriate) condition that the offended ones themselves choose to lay down. </span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-bottom:0in;text-align:justify;text-indent:
1.0in'><span style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'>Besides, perfection is
always the standard of behavior between persons, and (b) perfection once broken
cannot be overcome. </span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-bottom:0in;text-align:justify;text-indent:
1.0in'><span style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'>Finally, we may even add
another way of looking at it: (c) by sinning we have identified ourselves with
the Adam group which as a group is separated from God by spiritual death as
pictured graphically by physical death (1 Corinthians <b>15</b>:22; cp. Romans<b>
5</b>:12-21).</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-bottom:0in;text-align:justify;text-indent:
.5in'><span style='font-size:12.0pt;line-height:115%'>So, it is inherently
impossible for persons to save themselves (autosoterism).</span></p>
