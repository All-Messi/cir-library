<p><em>“For the joy that was set before him, he endured the cross”</em> (Hebrews <strong>12</strong>:2). It was the power of joy that Jesus could profit from. More exactly it was the power of the hope for joy, a joy not yet experienced, that carried him through what he was experiencing: being crucified.</p>
<p>Even more, his crucifixion came, not as a tragedy that overtook him unbidden, but one he saw coming early on (Matthew<strong> 16</strong>:21). He bore the shadow of a cross every day we walked. On the last day he willingly submitted to a cross that was heavier than he could even carry (John <strong>18</strong>:6).</p>
<p>Yet more still, that future joy was not just to his own benefit—as in relief from agony; it was a suffering for the benefit of others—us. The features of that cross event play out in our experience who pick up crosses and follow him to Calvary. That hope for joy empowers us to persevere.</p>
<p>When it was over for him, he <em>“sat down on the right hand of God.”</em> And if we <em>“endure with h</em><em>i</em><em>m, we will also reign with him” </em>(2 Timothy <strong>2</strong>:12). These bread and wine emblems of his endurance put us in connection with the whole process that leads disciples of Jesus to the right hand of God.</p>
