<p><strong><sup>5</sup></strong>Take the attitude Christ Jesus took.</p>
<p><strong><sup>	</sup></strong><strong><sup>6</sup></strong>Although he was in the form of deity,</p>
<p>he didn’t consider being on a par with God</p>
<p>something to hang onto.</p>
<p><strong><sup>	</sup></strong><strong><sup>7</sup></strong>He emptied himself,</p>
<p>and took the place of a servant.</p>
<p>In the form of a man,</p>
<p><strong><sup>8</sup></strong>he humbled himself to the point of dying—</p>
<p>dying on a cross.</p>
<p><strong><sup>	</sup></strong><strong><sup>9</sup></strong>So God lifted him up above all others</p>
<p><strong><sup>10</sup></strong>so every knee in heaven, on earth, and under the earth</p>
<p>would kneel to him;</p>
<p><strong><sup>	</sup></strong><strong><sup>11</sup></strong>and every tongue would acknowledge Jesus Christ as Lord—</p>
<p>to the glory of God the Father.  	(Philippians <strong>2</strong>:5-10)</p>
<p>In his “humility” he accepted limitation. Instead of saying, <em>“Let there be</em>,<em>”</em><em> </em>he would need to use means to produce results—like a loaf of bread: plow the ground, plant the seed, water the soil, weed the field, harvest, the crop, thresh out the grain, prepare the lump, cook it in an oven, and after three months take a bite.</p>
<p>He would no longer exercise sovereignty; he would submit to parents (Luke <strong>2</strong>:51), and as oldest son work as “a builder” responsible for a family of eight (Mark<strong> 6</strong>:3). He would accept discomfort like having to walk all the way to Sychar—getting tired, thirsty, and hungry (John <strong>4</strong>:1-58). He would feel the cold of the night (John <strong>18</strong>:18), the heat of the day, the gnaw of grief (John <strong>11</strong>:35; Luke <strong>19</strong>:41), and six hours of agony in the end (Mark <strong>15</strong>:25; Matthew <strong>27</strong>:45-50).</p>
<p>He would put up with opposition without using his authority to shut people up in their disrespect, rejection, mockery, and persecution; and without appealing to an army of angels to protect him from death’s defeat (Matthew<strong> 26</strong>:53).</p>
<p>What humility means is clear from his example of it in self-limitation, clearer from his extreme example like submitting to a cross, but clearest from the Judas alternative by contrast—who betrayed a friend with kiss to get hold of thirty coins (Matthew <strong>26</strong>:14).</p>
<p>If we take the “attitude” Jesus took as pictured in these emblems, we will participate in a pattern of “exaltation after humility” (Philippians<strong> 2</strong>:8-9) instead of self-destruction by our own doing (cp. Matthew <strong>27</strong>:5). In this exercise we act out what was embodied in the Lord we honor.</p>
